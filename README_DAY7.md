In the program, they personalize things by linking the energy use on campus to how many students are at the university. Its sort of based on this idea that each student uses about 0.1 units of energy around there. I think that assumption makes sense for figuring out if the total energy adds up reasonably for a school that size.

The way they calculate the expected amount is pretty straightforward. You just multiply the number of students by 0.1. That gives you what it should be roughly.

Then after getting the actual energy from all the readings that count, the program checks it against that expected number. If the real consumption is less than or equal to what was expected, then its fine, like the usage matches the population okay.

But if its more, that means they are using extra energy beyond what the students would normally take. Which points to maybe some inefficiency or something.

This whole approach feels more real because it takes the universitys size into account when looking at how efficient the campus is with energy. Not just raw numbers, but tied to the people there. Though I am not totally sure how accurate that 0.1 figure is in every case.
